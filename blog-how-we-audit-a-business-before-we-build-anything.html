How We Audit a Business Before We Build Anything

HT
Harry Thornberry 1760 Strategic AI

Most founders come to us wanting to build something. A new system, a new workflow, an AI tool, a sales process. They have a solution in mind before they have diagnosed the constraint. We do not start there. Every engagement at 1760 Strategic AI begins with an audit. Not a discovery call. Not a proposal. A diagnostic.


Why We Audit First


Building without auditing is how you end up with expensive solutions to the wrong problems. We have seen it repeatedly — a company installs a CRM they do not need, automates a process that should be eliminated, or hires for a gap that is not actually causing the constraint. The audit exists to prevent that.


The audit answers one question: where is the business actually losing leverage? That answer almost never matches what the founder thought it was when they first reached out.


What We Look For


The audit covers four areas:


Revenue flow — where leads come from, how they convert, and where deals stall

Operational load — which tasks are eating time that should not be, and what is running on tribal knowledge

Team structure — who owns what, where accountability is unclear, and what is falling between roles

Technology stack — what tools are in use, what they are actually being used for, and where there is friction or absence

We are not looking for everything wrong with the business. We are looking for the one or two places where fixing something would create the most downstream leverage.


The 30-Minute Diagnostic


The initial audit takes 30 minutes. That is not a teaser — it is genuinely enough time to identify the primary constraint if you ask the right questions in the right order.


We start with revenue because revenue problems are visible fastest. Then we move to operations, because most revenue problems have an operational root cause. Then we look at accountability, because most operational problems are really ownership problems.


By the end of 30 minutes we have a working hypothesis about where the constraint is. It is not a finished analysis. It is a direction.


What Comes Out of the Audit


The output is not a report. It is a prioritized list of two or three interventions that would move the business forward fastest, with a clear rationale for why those and not the ten other things we noticed.


From there, the founder decides whether to act on it themselves, engage us to build the solution, or do nothing. We have no preference. The audit is designed to give clarity, not to create a dependency.


Why Most Audits Fail


Most consultants run discovery calls that are really sales calls. They are gathering information to justify a proposal, not to find the actual constraint. The questions are broad, the listening is shallow, and the output is a scope of work that reflects what the consultant already planned to sell.


We run the audit the other way. We start with no predetermined solution. We go where the data goes. If the constraint is something we cannot help with, we say so.


How to Apply This


If you are evaluating any outside firm, ask them what their diagnostic process looks like before they propose anything. If they cannot describe a specific sequence of questions and a clear output, they are selling before diagnosing.


If you want to run a version of this on your own business, start with one question: where does work slow down or stop before it produces revenue? Follow that thread. It will lead you to the constraint faster than any framework.


The 30-minute AI Audit we run at 1760 Strategic AI follows this same sequence. Book one if you want an outside set of eyes on where your business is losing leverage.
